## Strengths

- The question is concrete and names checkable facts: the store Santa, the reindeer sleigh and NORAD.
- It correctly points to NORAD, which is a real organisation with a real Santa-tracking tradition, so part of the premise can be confirmed.

## Objections

### O1 [major]

The proposal presents the conclusion "so he exists" as following from seeing Santa in a department store. That inference is false. The man in the store is an actor or seasonal employee in costume, and children sitting on his lap is a commercial and cultural tradition. It is no evidence of a real person who delivers gifts. A factual answer must not confirm the premise.

Suggestion: Answer directly that Santa Claus as a person who flies around the world with reindeer and delivers gifts does not exist. Explain that the store Santa is someone portraying the character. Mention the historical roots: St. Nicholas of Myra, the Dutch Sinterklaas, and the 19th-century American literary and advertising image (Clement Moore's poem, Thomas Nast's illustrations).

### O2 [major]

The claim that NORAD tracks Santa is only half true and misleading as phrased. The NORAD Tracks Santa program is real. It began in 1955, when a Sears Roebuck ad misprinted a phone number and children called the CONAD command centre; Col. Harry Shoup answered them playfully. NORAD, founded in 1958, took it over. Today it is a public-outreach tradition run by volunteers, with a website, phone line and apps. It is not a military detection of a real sleigh.

Suggestion: State that NORAD Tracks Santa is a real annual event, with the 1955 Sears misprint as its origin. Make clear that it is a playful tradition, not radar tracking of a real flight. Add that it is not limited to the USA: the tracking covers the whole world, and NORAD is a joint US–Canadian command.

### O3 [minor]

The claim that Santa delivers gifts in the USA is too narrow and ignores the Czech context of the question. Gift-bringing traditions differ by country. In the Czech Republic, Ježíšek (the Baby Jesus) brings gifts on 24 December, and Mikuláš visits on 5 December.

Suggestion: Add one sentence noting that the gift-giver differs by culture (Santa Claus in the USA, Ježíšek in Czechia, Mikuláš on 5 December). Also say that Santa's gift-giving is a myth shared by many countries, not something tied to US airspace.

### O4 [minor]

The question does not say who is asking or whether a child may read the answer. The answer should be factually honest without needlessly spoiling the belief for children.

Suggestion: Open with a short, tactful, factually correct answer, such as "the character is a legend, not a real person". Add a note that if children are reading, the user may prefer to adapt the tone.
